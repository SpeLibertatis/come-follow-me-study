STORY 1 OF 3 · ABOUT 4 MINUTES

01

Daniel chooses

Narrative context · original summary

Daniel was far from home and under pressure to adapt. Where did he draw a line?

Read Daniel 1:8–17 ↗

What did Daniel decide before knowing the outcome?

Source-led discovery · reading prompts

Notice the pressure

Read Daniel 1:8–10. What request does Daniel make, and what does the official fear?

Consider the response

Read Daniel 1:11–14. Notice the proposed test before looking at its result.

Read the outcome

Read Daniel 1:15–17. What does this account report? It does not promise the same result in every trial.

STORY 2 OF 3 · ABOUT 4 MINUTES

02

But if not

Interpretation · consider alongside the source

The friends trusted God's power without demanding a particular outcome.

Read Daniel 3:13–18 ↗

What does “but if not” teach about faith?

Source-led discovery · reading prompts

Notice the command

Read Daniel 3:13–15. What choice does the king demand?

Consider the response

Read Daniel 3:16–18. How do the friends make room for an outcome without rescue?

Read the outcome

Read Daniel 3:19–28. This narrative describes a rescue; Daniel 3:18 also gives room for faith when rescue does not come.

STORY 3 OF 3 · ABOUT 4 MINUTES

03

Keep praying

Interpretation · consider alongside the source

Daniel's faith was strengthened through consistent habits.

Read Daniel 6:7–10, 16–23 ↗

What prepared Daniel to keep praying?

Source-led discovery · reading prompts

Notice the pressure

Read Daniel 6:7–9. What does the new law forbid?

Consider the response

Read Daniel 6:10. What does Daniel continue doing?

Read the outcome

Read Daniel 6:16–23. Notice the narrative outcome without treating it as a guarantee that faithful people will avoid suffering.

What did you discover?

All three stories show faith under pressure. Faith does not guarantee an easy outcome.
