CONTEXT · ORIGINAL SUMMARY

Amaziah, a priest at Beth-el, tells Amos to leave and stop prophesying there. Amos answers by describing his ordinary work with flocks and fruit, then the Lord’s call.

Read Amos 7:12–15 ↗
“And the Lord took me as I followed the flock”

Amos 7:15 · KJV excerpt

What does Amaziah want Amos to do? What reason does Amos give for speaking anyway?

Look at the contrast

Amaziah appeals to the king’s sanctuary and court (verse 13). Amos points to the Lord’s call (verse 15). His answer is not a claim to greater popularity or a more impressive background.

“Herdman” means someone who tends livestock. Amos also describes gathering sycomore fruit.

02THE SOURCE

A voice with a source.

Earlier in the book, Amos used familiar sounds and events to make people think about what lies behind them.

Read Amos 3:3–4, 7–8 ↗

7 Surely the Lord God will do nothing, but he revealeth his secret unto his servants the prophets.

8 The lion hath roared, who will not fear? the Lord God hath spoken, who can but prophesy?

Amos 3:7–8 · King James Version

In verse 8, what explains why Amos speaks? How does verse 7 change the way you hear his warning?

Notice the source before opening an explanation.

The source

The Lord has spoken. Amos connects his message to God, not to his own importance.

The messenger

Amos has been called to prophesy. The roar comparison explains why he cannot simply keep quiet.

The listener

An unwelcome message still deserves careful attention. Ask what the Lord is revealing, not only whether the message is comfortable.

Prophetic truth can warn, teach, and give hope. A warning can create an opportunity to turn toward the Lord; it is not the whole of a prophet’s message.

Optional · follow the full chain in Amos 3:3–8 ↗

In this passage, “secret” points to what the Lord reveals to His prophets. These verses are not a promise that we will know every future event or the reason for every hardship.

ONE MODERN EXAMPLE · OFFICIAL CHURCH TEACHING

The weekly lesson links to Elder Michael John U. Teh, whose talk links to President Dallin H. Oaks’s “Following Christ.” One invitation in that message is to give up contention as we follow Jesus.

President Oaks · Following Christ ↗

In section III, read the paragraph containing “We need to love and do good to all” and the final paragraph of the message. Then read the Savior’s teaching:

Read 3 Nephi 11:29–30 ↗

What does Jesus ask us to stop stirring up?

Try it in a fictional group chat · optional

Someone posts an embarrassing screenshot. People you like pile on.

What can you do without adding to the contention?

Think of one response before opening the possibilities below. You do not need to describe a real incident.

Consider a few possibilities

Don’t forward it. Ask people to stop sharing it. Check on the person being hurt. If there is bullying, a threat, or something unsafe, ask a trusted adult for help.

Peacemaking is not pretending harm is okay. You can disagree, set a boundary, or seek help without joining the attack.

The Lord sent Amos to speak while people still had a chance to change. President Oaks points back to Jesus’s words for a choice we can make today. Even if you already forwarded the screenshot, it isn’t too late to change what you do next.
